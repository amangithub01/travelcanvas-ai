# Past-trip interview: 20 minutes

**Goal:** understand actual planning behavior before discussing this product.
Use the consent and handling rules in [the kit guide](README.md). Do not pitch
the app or describe its budget features before the main questions.

## 0 to 3 minutes: context

Confirm they personally planned a solo domestic trip within six months. Ask:

> Roughly when was it, how many nights, and what parts did you organize yourself?

Record broad context only. If ineligible, explain politely; any exploratory
conversation should be labeled outside the primary sample.

## 3 to 12 minutes: reconstruct what happened

Ask in order, following the participant's story rather than a rigid checklist:

1. Walk me through that trip, from deciding to travel to having a plan.
2. What did you do first? What happened next?
3. Which tools, notes, or messages did you switch between?
4. Where did you get stuck or have to redo something?
5. What costs did you discover later than expected? What did you change?
6. What information did you check elsewhere before trusting it?

Useful neutral follow-ups:

> Can you describe one specific occasion?
>
> What did you do when that happened?
>
> What was the consequence?
>
> How do you know that took that much time?

If they offer to show an artifact, let them redact it and avoid collecting copies.
Do not request booking references, private messages, or payment information.
Record whether a time/cost figure is recalled, documented, or your estimate.

## 12 to 17 minutes: alternatives and consequences

1. Which part was hardest, if any?
2. What did you try to make that part easier?
3. Have you actually paid someone or a service for planning help? What happened?
4. Did you abandon or simplify a plan? What caused that?
5. What worked well enough that you would not want to change it?

Accept "nothing was difficult" as evidence. Do not rescue the hypothesis by
suggesting problems, asking whether AI sounds useful, or requesting a rating of
an idea they have not used.

## 17 to 20 minutes: close

> What important part of planning have I missed?

Summarize what you heard without adding a product solution and let them correct
it. Ask separately whether they would welcome a future usability invitation.
Do not interpret permission to contact them as product demand.

Afterward, log 3 to 5 evidence rows: concrete event, consequence, workaround,
trust check, and contradictory evidence where present. Keep the participant's
words distinct from your interpretation. Mark whether each point emerged
independently or only after you prompted that topic.
